Pixel Veil help

Pixel Veil covers the parts of a photo you want to keep private, such as faces, names, addresses and licence plates. It runs in this browser and nowhere else.

How to use it

  1. Open one photo or many. You can also drop photos onto the page or paste from the clipboard.
  2. Drag a box over anything you want hidden.
  3. Press Save. You get new files and your original photos stay as they were. On an iPhone or iPad the share sheet opens. Choose Save Image to put the copy in Photos.

The tools

Auto
Chooses for you. A long, thin box (a line of text, across or down) is blurred. Any other box is pixelated.
Pixelate
Turns the area into large blocks. Strength sets the block size.
Blur
Smooths the area until the detail is gone. Strength sets how soft it gets.
Black
Covers the area with solid black.
Reveal
Drag backwards, from the bottom right up to the top left, to bring the original back inside that box.
Faces
Finds the faces in the photo and marks every one to be hidden. Tap the faces you want to keep visible, then press Apply. It uses Blur or Black if that tool is selected, and pixelates otherwise.
Crop
Drag out the part to keep. Move the box or pull its edges, then press Apply. Save waits until you have pressed Apply or Cancel.
Revert
Goes back to the photo as you opened it. Undo steps back through your changes, and it can undo a Revert too.

Check the faces yourself

Face detection can miss people. Small faces, faces turned away and faces that are partly covered are the usual ones. Look over each photo before you save it, and drag a box over any face that was missed.

Several photos at once

Open more than one photo and a strip of thumbnails appears under the picture. Click a thumbnail to work on that photo. Hold Ctrl or ⌘ and click to add photos to the selection, or hold Shift and click to select a run. From the keyboard, hold Shift and press the arrow keys to select a run, or hold Ctrl (⌘) and use the arrow keys to move, then Ctrl (⌘) + Space to add or remove a photo. On a touch screen, press Select and then tap the photos, or press and hold a thumbnail.

While two or more photos are selected, every edit goes onto all of them at the same place in each photo. A drag, a crop and a Revert all work this way, and Undo takes the edit back off all of them. The line under the tools, or the count beside the photos on a small screen, says how many photos you are editing.

Faces is different, because each photo has different people in it. It walks through the selected photos one at a time so you can choose the faces in each. You can also hide every face in every selected photo in a single step.

A dot on a thumbnail means the photo has edits. A tick means it has been saved since its last edit.

Saving

Save offers JPEG (a smaller file) or PNG (lossless, best for screenshots). The file is named after your photo with _anonymized added. Every photo is saved at its own full size.

With several photos open you can save this photo, the selected photos or all of them. Several photos are saved together as one ZIP file in your downloads. An iPhone or iPad offers the share sheet first, with the ZIP as the other choice.

Each saved file is built fresh from the pixels of the edited photo. Nothing else from the original comes along, so location, camera details, dates and other metadata (Exif and GPS) are left out. That is true for photos you did not edit as well.

Privacy

Your photos are never uploaded. All the work happens on this device. There are no accounts, no tracking and no analytics. The only network requests are for the app's own files, from the place it is hosted. The face detector is one of those files, and it is fetched the first time you use Faces unless the offline copy is already saved. The single-file version makes no requests at all.

Your tool, strength and format choices are kept in this browser so they are there next time. Photos are never stored.

Using it offline

After the first visit the app works with no connection. To install it, choose Install from the address bar or browser menu in Chrome or Edge. On an iPhone or iPad, use Share → Add to Home Screen. There is also a single-file version that opens straight from disk.

A caution about text

Blurred or pixelated text can sometimes be reconstructed, especially short strings such as numbers. Use Black for anything truly sensitive, such as passwords, account numbers and addresses.
